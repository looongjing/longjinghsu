I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

I build conversational robots for people living with dementia and study what changes once those robots are in use, in memory-care facilities, senior centers, and private homes. I have co-designed and deployed these systems with more than 60 older adults, people living with dementia, and care partners.

I am an Eric and Wendy Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Michigan, working with Patricia Alves-Oliveira and Joyce Chai. In my current project, dementia-care experts annotated more than 200 first-person accounts from people living with dementia, and I use those annotations to fine-tune language models that guide a robot’s conversations. Before Michigan, I spent four years at Indiana University running a robot design program with older adults around IRIS, a robot that supports ikigai, a sense of purpose, in partnership with the Toyota Research Institute.

I received my Ph.D. in Informatics (human-computer interaction track, minor in gerontology) from Indiana University, advised by Selma Šabanović and Christina Chung. My work has received an Honorable Mention at ACM DIS and a Best Video Award at ACM/IEEE HRI, and has been covered by Wired and NBC News.
